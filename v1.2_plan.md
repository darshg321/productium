# Productium v1.2 plan

v1.2 turns Productium from a study planner that also tracks some time into an **AI-native record of where every
minute goes**. Planning stays, but it sits on top of one precise time log.

The idea everything follows from: **anything with a time can be moved, skipped, logged and counted the same way.**
So the whole app is time entries (past, running, future), with deadline-driven tasks feeding the future and the
phone's sensors feeding the past. The user's job is that every minute of the day is accounted for, wasted time
included (what exactly you were doing while wasting it). The app's job is to make that nearly effortless and turn it
into data that makes you more productive. Every other feature (plan, ring, goals, rewards, patterns, questions) is
built on that log.

What the app is, in order of importance:

1. **The box.** One text box that can do anything: log what you did ("chem at the library 3 to 5"), start and stop
   things ("starting shower", "done"), answer questions with charts ("how much math in the last 28 days?"), and
   change tasks, schedule, alarms and settings.
2. **Start buttons.** One tap starts tracking something you do often. The AI makes them; you never configure them.
3. **Tasks.** The AI makes and edits them. You can start them, mark them done, or clear / delete them if they were
   never done. Starting a task and tapping a start button are the same action, so as the plan gets better, buttons
   turn into tasks.

Everything else (the ring chart, Review, notifications, rewards) reads the time log. Every screen element has one job,
and nothing appears twice.

Goals:

0. **One model, two lanes (Part 0).** Everything that happens at a time (classes, events, day changes, task
   sessions, sleep, alarms, reminders, the log) becomes one `Entry` type, with `Task` and `Rule` beside it. That
   replaces 15 overlapping box entities with 7 nouns. Everyday actions get strict, tested paths, often with no AI.
   Odd situations get Sonnet's judgement and real freedom, limited only by a few checks on what could hurt, and
   all of it can be undone.
1. **One precise time log (Part B).** Every stretch of time is an `Entry`. It can be live (start / stop), told
   afterwards ("between 3 and 5 I was …"), taken from your school timetable, or inferred from sensors. It can be as
   precise as a single shower.
2. **A category tree the AI keeps tidy (Part C).** Two or three levels, e.g. Work › Extracurricular › Volleyball,
   Unproductive › Scrolling, Free Time › Making App, School › Math. The AI files each entry, a background pass merges
   and renames categories as new kinds of entries arrive, and your corrections teach it.
3. **Live tracking with start buttons, tasks and a running notification (Part D).**
4. **Location all the time (Part E):** each entry knows where it happened, and Review shows where your time goes.
5. **Ask anything about your time, get a chart (Part F).** The AI writes a query, code runs it on the phone, and a
   chart is drawn from the result. The model never writes the numbers itself.
6. **A home screen built around the day's ring chart (Part G).** The box, start buttons and mood buttons, the ring
   chart, then Up Next. The rest of today's ring is a forecast from your recent similar days, so you can beat your
   usual day (more study, less scrolling, more sleep). Review has the same ring for day / week / month with
   drill-down, and it replaces As It Went.
7. **Tasks that learn from what you finish and what you drop (Part H)**, with advice when you add something you
   usually drop, and reminders that actually motivate.
8. **Rewards that work (Part I):** a built-in goal of no untracked stretch longer than 30 minutes, beating your
   forecast, time goals per category and task-completion goals, closed on the ring, with Life Metrics more prominent.
9. **Full-screen alarms only with your approval (Part J).**
10. **Fix the bugs (Part A):** the AI couldn't delete an event it made, sleep doesn't load reliably, and "1/5" and
    "2/5" of the same task were shown as two different kinds of thing.
11. **Simpler (Part K):** remove the manual "+" forms, flashcards and reads, the Schedule and Places settings, the
    Rest of Today numbers, the check-in card and the old Focus card. They're absorbed into the parts above.
12. **Use everything a free account can reach (Part M):** Focus filters, Shortcuts triggers (NFC stickers, Back Tap,
    Bluetooth, sleep), intent donations, Photos (automatic Screen Time screenshots), on-device OCR and Apple's
    on-device model, more Health signals, Lock Screen controls if the extension spike works, and Reminders /
    Calendar mirrors as free stand-ins for widgets. All of it serves the time log: fewer prompts, better guesses,
    one-tap starts from anywhere.
13. **Assumed time is accurate until you say no.** Classes, calendar events, things you said you'd do and planned
    routines count as what happened. A quick yes / no through the day (and the end-of-day review) turns assumed into
    confirmed, or corrects it. Planned study is never assumed: it's tracked or it's missed (Part B, Part I).
14. **The schedule is driven by deadlines, and judged by the day's total.** "Test in a week" spreads study across the
    week. A day is only short when you did less task time than it asked for, in any order (`Accountability`).
15. **The AI can change anything, past included.** Big or risky changes wait for one Approve / Decline; nothing is
    refused for being unusual (Part 0.3).
16. **Patterns, unasked (Part P):** the insight pass reads the log in the background and tells you what it notices.
17. **Telemetry in every backup (Part T)**, so problems found while using the app can be traced from the export.
18. **Notice what happened (Part N):** the phone's data (location, motion, steps, alarm stops, pickups, workouts)
    logs time by itself when it's sure, and asks one sharp question when the plan and reality disagree ("You're home,
    but school's on until 3. What happened?").

Overall order: **T1–T2** (telemetry first, so everything after is observable) → **A0 + A5–A9** (hotfix, sleep, notification replies, shippable as v1.1.x) → **Part 0 O1–O10** (the one model; a clean
break, old types are deleted as each caller moves over) → **B1–B6** (the log everything else reads) → **C1–C4** →
**D** (with **M1–M4**: the free triggers that start and stop entries) → **G1–G4** (the ring on Home as soon as there's
data) → **E** → **N** (needs E's places and Part 0's expanded days) → **F** → **H** → **I** → **J** → **K** (removals run alongside the part that replaces each feature) →
**C5–C7** (the background pass needs a few days of real entries) → **M5–M12** → **L**.

Status at the start: v1.1.0 released. Core tests green on Linux. `App/` is compiled only by CI (`ios-compile`), and
v1.1 Part B (testing on the iPhone itself) never ran, so every App-side task below ends with "checked on the phone by
you", not just CI.

## Part 0: One model (time entries, tasks, rules) and two lanes for the AI

### 0.0 Why

v1.1 had **15 box entities** (task, activity, event, day_change, situation, alarm, reminder, day_rule, gap, marker,
mood, screen_time, routine, plus calendar events and plan blocks the model could see but not touch), **30 knobs**,
a `split` macro, and situation effects as JSON inside strings. The same fact could be said in three to five ways,
and the model kept picking a way the code couldn't find again.

The 2026-11-18 backup (a real day of use) shows what that cost. Of 27 prompts:

| What you said | What v1.1 did | v1.2 |
|---|---|---|
| The timetable, with P1/P2 and P3/P4 swapped on even dates | 3 turns; the first failed (it guessed the ref "aschool"), the swap was a `replace` situation | 10 repeating entries (odd / even `DayRule`s) in one turn; spares and lunch `open` |
| "too much reading today… no more than 45m reading a day" | split one task into 5 tasks with made-up due dates | one task, `maxMinutesPerDay: 45` |
| "spare periods should be used for easy work" | an `atSchool` flag on four tasks | spares are `open` time; the planner puts only light work there |
| "data quiz monday… you already have data period time so that's when it is" | due 08:00, then 13:26 by hand | due = the start of Monday's Data occurrence, which is in the context |
| "no cs club meeting tomorrow" | refused (calendar is read-only), made a `free` situation | skip that calendar occurrence (Calendar itself is never written) |
| "starting dayoneof review" … "done" | a start marker that never ended, then task done | `task start` → a running entry with its `taskId`; `task done` stops it |
| "actually i just finished just now" | "already done, nothing to change" | change the entry's end |
| "didn't do valorant today" / "why can't you edit it" | "I can't edit the past" | skip it: the past is editable |
| "frankenstein readings are ordered wrong and too many" | fiddling with 5 fake tasks | doesn't arise: one task, stable "n of m" |
| "brag sheet today is only 15 mins" (twice) | the plan still showed five 20-minute brag blocks | one change |
| Insight pass "starter steps" | extra tasks that then didn't fit | `firstStep` on the task |

All of these are replayed in `BoxOpsTests.testBackupPromptsAreExpressible` as the ops Sonnet should send, and they
become the first `inbox-eval` cases (O9).

### 0.1 The model

```swift
/// A stretch of time: past (it happened, or is assumed to), now (running), or future (it will). Once or repeating.
struct Entry {
  var id, title, start, end?               // end nil = running now (one at a time)
  var category: CategoryID, filedByYou, topics, placeId?, note?   // note: what ran on the side, why, where exactly
  var taskId: UUID?                        // time for a task
  var repeats: Repeat?                     // any DayRule (weekdays, odd/even dates, A/B, labels…), from–until
  var marks: [DayKey: Mark]                // per occurrence: skipped | confirmed
  var open: Bool                           // you're there but it isn't busy (spare, lunch): light work can go in
  var alert: Alert?                        // notify / alarm, before start or end (wake = alarm at the end of Sleep)
  var source: you | calendar | health | sensor | plan
}
struct Task { title, due, minutes, done minutes, kind, difficulty, importance, category?, notBefore?,
              maxMinutesPerDay?, firstStep?, state: open | done | dropped }      // `WorkItem` in code
struct Rule { text, said?, from?, until?, days?, limits: PlannerLimits }         // how the planner fills days
```

Deliberately small:

- **One way to edit one day of a series:** skip it, or *detach* it (the series is skipped that day and the day
  becomes its own one-off entry, edited like any other). "Late start Thursday", "teacher moved Math", "CS Club at 12
  instead" are all that.
- **Busy is what it is:** everything that isn't the plan's own (or a sensor's) is time the planner works around,
  unless it's `open`. No `fixed` / `pinned` flags. Moving a planned block makes it yours, so it stays.
- **Overlaps are settled by what kind of time it is** (B.0), not by flags.
- **Assumed vs confirmed** (B.0): scheduled things, sensor time and planned routines are assumed (accurate unless
  you say no); planned study is never assumed.
- **Rules are only for the planner.** Something to remember goes to About Me; something with a time is an entry;
  something about a task is a task field. A Rule is for "exam week: 45 minute blocks", "no study after 9:45 on school
  nights", "wake at 7 on Friday", "no reminders tomorrow".

| v1.1 | v1.2 |
|---|---|
| Activity (`Commitment`) + timetable `periods` | one repeating entry per period (School › Math …; spare / lunch `open`) |
| One-off event (`oneOffs`) | an entry |
| Calendar event | an entry with source `calendar`, id from the EventKit id; skip = hide; change = skip + your own entry |
| Day change: no school, late start | skip / detach that day ("no school Friday" = skip Friday's School) |
| Situation `busy` / `free` / `replace` / `place` | entries / skips / detached days / a session entry with `taskId` |
| Situation `setInput`, `mute`, `hold` / `boost`, `note` | `Rule.limits` / `Rule` / `notBefore`, importance / About Me |
| Day rules (labels, sequences) | inside `Repeat` (A/B days stay possible) |
| Marker, outcome, Focus session, gap answer | past or running entries |
| Routine (get ready, commute, dinner) | entries: yours ("dinner 7:30" = a daily Dinner entry) or the planner's |
| Plan blocks (`PlannedBlock`) | future entries with source `plan`, regenerated each replan |
| Sleep, wake, leave-by | entries (planned Sleep with the wake alarm on its end) |
| Alarm rules, reminders | `alert` on an entry, or a task with a time |
| Gap note ("couldn't do") | telemetry `gap` event (Part T) |
| 30 knobs | ~12 settings the student changes; planner knobs become `Rule.limits` or stay with the insight pass |

### 0.2 The box

- Nouns: `entry`, `task`, `rule`, `setting`, `mood`, `screen_time`, `query`. Refs `e…`, `e…@yyyy-MM-dd` (one
  occurrence), `t…`, `u…`.
- Verbs: `add`, `change`, `remove`, `start`, `stop`, `skip`, `confirm`, `done`, `drop`.
- Values are open: titles, `A › B › C` category paths (created as needed), any `DayRule`, notes, rule text.
- The context is **the days themselves**: today ±, the next 7 days as entry lines with refs (past ones marked
  assumed / confirmed, the running one, planned ones), open tasks, active rules, the category tree as `id name`
  lines. What the model sees is exactly what it can edit.
- The model can also answer with a question, or say what it couldn't do (`gap`, Part T) instead of guessing.

### 0.3 Two lanes, and trust

**Everyday lane: fixed, tested, often no AI.** Start, stop / done, log "X from 3 to 5", add a task with a due date,
mark done, move to tomorrow, skip, confirm, mood. One verb each, code-filled defaults (category from
`CategoryRules`, numbering, overlap trimming). Start buttons, notification actions, Shortcuts / NFC and the local
fast path (`QuickParse`) don't reach Sonnet at all. One worked example per verb in the prompt; 100% on the eval set.

**Exception lane: Sonnet's judgement, no refusals.** It can change any day, past or future: add, move, retime,
skip, detach, put task sessions at exact times, add repeating things with any day rule, write Rules, correct what
was logged. **The code doesn't refuse anything for being unusual.** It holds a change for one **Approve / Decline**
when it would:

1. remove a whole repeating series,
2. remove or overwrite an hour or more of time you logged yourself, or
3. add a full-screen alarm (Part J uses the same Approve).

Everything else applies at once, with the **day-by-day receipt** ("Fri 21 Nov: School skipped · Reading 2 of 3 at
10:00") and one-tap Undo. Sleep below your floor is applied with a warning line. If one kind of exception goes wrong
repeatedly in the evals, it gets a worked example in the prompt, not a refusal.

## Part A: Bugs

### A.1 The AI made an event but couldn't delete it

Causes found in `DumpApplier.swift`:

- `eventIndex` (`DumpApplier.swift:184`) looks up events by name only among **future** ones (`end > now`). Once an
  event has started, or is earlier today, it can't be found to delete or change.
- What looks like an "event" on screen can be four different things: a one-off (`oneOffs`), a situation's `busy`
  block, a situation's `place` block, or an imported calendar event. Delete only handles one-offs. The others answer
  "can't find event (calendar events are changed in Calendar)", even though the AI made them.
- The context lists at most 20 future one-offs (`DumpPrompt.swift:187`) and doesn't show which situation produced a
  busy block, so the model has no id to send back.

Fix (built as part of Part 0, where every block is an Entry with one ref; only A0 ships earlier): **every block
on screen gets one address the AI can use.** The context lists each shown block with a ref that
leads back to where it came from: `e…` one-off, `s…/busy` situation block, `t…/session` task session, `cal…` calendar
event. A delete on that ref removes the right thing: the one-off; the situation effect (or the whole situation if
that was its only effect); the task session; or, for a calendar event, an `ignoredEvents` entry ("hidden from the
plan; it's still in Calendar"). Look-up by name covers all of today and the next 14 days.

### A.2 "Reading 1/5" moved after 2/5, and 2/5 looked like a school calendar event

Causes:

- Study time for one task can come from three places: the planner's own work blocks, a situation `place` effect (a
  fixed `.work` block, `Planner.swift:231`), or a one-off event / situation `busy` block the model made to "move"
  something. Only the planner's blocks are renumbered in `finish()` (`Planner.swift:681`). The others keep plain titles
  or the model's own "2/5" title, and render as fixed (gray, school-looking) time that can't be cleared.
- There's no way to say "not today". Asked to move "Reading 1/5" to tomorrow, the model had nothing that fits, so
  it moved one block late in the day and left the others alone.
- Numbering comes from the order of the blocks, so each replan renumbers them. "1/5" isn't a fixed thing you can
  point at.

Fix (built as part of Part 0: task sessions are Entries with a `taskId`, so there's nothing else they could be):
**study time for a task is only ever a task session.**

- Sessions the AI or you put at a time are entries with the task's `taskId` (replaces `place` effects for tasks)
  and `notBefore: Date?` ("not today", "start Thursday"). The planner fills the rest of the task as now.
- Numbering is stable and counts finished work: "Reading · 2 of 5" means one session is done and this is the next. The
  earliest remaining session always has the lowest remaining number, including sessions you placed yourself.
- Time for a task is always an entry with its `taskId`; the prompt's worked examples say so, and an entry whose title
  matches an open task gets linked to it (said in the receipt).
- Box verbs: "move X to tomorrow" sets `notBefore` to the start of tomorrow (or moves just that session if one was
  named); "do X at 4" pins a session; "skip today's" defers that session.
- Every task session renders the same way everywhere (task style, Start / Done / Clear), never as fixed time.

### A.3 Sleep doesn't load reliably

Seen in the 2026-11-20 backup: `health` has steps and nothing else, and `permissions` reports `health.sleep:
sharingDenied` (as do steps, workouts, resting heart rate). HealthKit only reports *write* status; read access is
never visible to the app, so these values don't say whether sleep can be read (steps were read fine while also
showing `sharingDenied`). So the likely cause is no sleep samples (no Watch / Sleep schedule) or the overwrite bug
below, not permissions. The first fix is to stop guessing:
every Health read records its sample count and error in telemetry (Part T), so the next backup shows whether sleep
is unreadable, empty, or overwritten.

Causes found in `HealthService.swift` and `AppModel.syncHealth`:

- **Overwrites good nights with empty ones.** `byDay[day] = d` (`AppModel.swift:530`) replaces a day that already had
  sleep with a fresh fetch that came back empty. That happens on every fetch while the phone is locked (HealthKit is
  unreadable until first unlock and on a locked device), after a failed read (`try?` → `[:]`), or when the Watch
  hasn't synced yet.
- **Only "asleep" samples count.** An iPhone with no Watch, or with only Sleep Focus / Bedtime, writes `inBed`. Those
  are filtered out, so there's no sleep at all.
- **Multiple sources are unioned** (Watch + phone + a third-party app). Intervals are merged, but a stray `inBed` or
  nap from one source stretches bed / wake.
- The night-building code is in `App/`, so it has never been tested.

Fix:

- Move night-building into core (`SleepNights.build(samples:[SleepSample], day:, calendar:)`) with fixtures: Watch
  only, phone only (`inBed`), both, a nap, an overnight gap, DST, a late sync.
- Rules: if a source has asleep stages, use the best single source per night (most asleep minutes); otherwise use
  `inBed`, marked "in bed (no stages)". Bed = first sleep sample that starts after 18:00 the evening before, wake =
  last sample that ends before 15:00. Naps (outside that window or under 20 minutes) are separate entries.
- Merge per field and never clear a value: a fetch that returns nothing for a day keeps what was there. A fetch error
  is recorded (`HealthDay.fetchError`, last success time) and shown in Settings → Sleep.
- Fetch on foreground, on `protectedDataDidBecomeAvailable`, from the observer query, and in background refresh;
  re-read the last 3 nights each time (late Watch syncs).
- When there's no source by 10:00: one notification "Slept about 11:20–7:05?" (from phone-use / motion estimate) with
  **Yes** / **Change** (Change opens the box). The answer becomes an `Entry` (Sleep).
- Sleep becomes `Entry`s (Parts 0 and B), so the ring and queries read it like any other time.

### A.5 Notifications that say "reply here" but can't take a reply

What the code does today (`NotificationService.swift`, `ProductiumApp.swift:39`):

- Only two categories have a text field: `CHECK_IN` (Reply) and `GAP` (Answer). Every other kind (start, lead-up,
  wind-down, bedtime, deadline, movement, intentions, scroll) has buttons or nothing. But the copy and the Shortcuts
  guide imply you can always just answer: "Reply here, no need to open the app" (`Nudges.swift:245`), "Say anything
  … without opening the app" (`ShortcutsGuideView.swift:318`).
- Even where there is a text field, the reply goes straight to `AppModel.dump(text)`, a full Sonnet call over
  Tailscale from a background launch. Nothing extends the background time, the text isn't saved first, and no
  result comes back. If Tailscale isn't up, the call is slow, or iOS suspends the app, **the reply is lost silently**.
  From the outside that looks the same as "you can't type into notifications".
- iOS only shows the text field after a long press / swipe-down on the notification. On a locked phone, iOS may ask
  you to unlock first. Nothing in the app explains this.

Fix:

- **Copy matches what's possible:** no notification text says or implies "reply" unless it has a text field. Every
  question-type notification ("What are you up to?", "Not at school?", "Slept about …?", the check-in) gets the
  text field *and* 2–3 one-tap answers, so typing is never the only way.
- **A reply is never lost:** save it first to an outbox (`AppState.outbox`, persisted before any network call).
  Then try O7's local fast path, which needs no network. Only after that call Sonnet, inside
  `beginBackgroundTask`. Unsent outbox items are sent on the next launch or background refresh.
- **A reply always gets an answer:** a quiet follow-up notification with the receipt ("Logged Shower 9:10–9:25") or
  "Saved, I'll handle it when you open Productium" if Sonnet couldn't be reached.
- A one-line tip the first time a question notification is sent: "Press and hold a notification to answer it".

| A9 | Outbox + local fast path first + `beginBackgroundTask` + retry on launch / refresh; follow-up receipt notification | `OutboxTests` (saved before send, retried, removed once applied); checked on the phone by you with Tailscale off |

## Part N: Notice what happened from the phone's data, and ask when it doesn't add up

### N.0 Principle

The phone already knows a lot about your day: where you are (E), how you're moving (`CMMotionActivity`), steps
(`CMPedometer` and Health), when alarms stop, when you first pick up the phone, when it's charging (the Shortcuts
automations), workouts, sleep. v1.2 turns these into **inferences** that either log time by themselves or ask one
sharp question. Most of all, it notices when **the plan and the evidence disagree**.

```swift
struct Inference {
  var what: Kind            // contradiction | walk | wake | commute | arrival | workout | stillness | …
  var interval: DateInterval
  var evidence: [Signal]    // "at Home 8:40–11:20", "School planned 8:30–15:00", "1,900 steps 7:05–7:25"
  var confidence: Double
  var proposal: Proposal    // the Entry (or skip / change) it would make
}
```

- **High confidence → logged by itself** as source `sensor`. It's hatched in the ring with one-tap Fix in the entry
  sheet, and it counts as tracked for the 30-minute goal.
- **Medium → ask** with the proposal as the first button ("Morning walk 7:05–7:25? · Yes · Something else").
- **Low → hint only:** it becomes one of the options in the next "What are you up to?" notification.
- Every answer teaches the thresholds per kind (`InferenceLearner`: confirm rate per kind and place). A kind you
  always confirm moves to auto-log; one you keep rejecting moves down to hints.

## Part B: One time log

### B.0 Principle

Today the time you actually spent is spread over six structures: `markers` (check-in start/end points),
`outcomes` ("how did it go"), `focus` (Start Focus), `health` sleep, scroll sessions, `motion`, plus the timetable,
which is assumed. `TimelineBuilder` paints them in layers into eight fixed categories. So you can't log "3 to 5 I was
at volleyball" precisely, can't correct a category, and can't ask detailed questions.

v1.2: **one log of intervals, everything else feeds it or reads from it.** The log is Part 0's `Entry` for the past
and the present (the same type the plan uses for the future). The word "time entry" below means an `Entry`.
`TimeLog.past` is the one function every screen, total, goal, query and the insight pass use for "what happened".

- **One active thing at a time.** What runs on the side (music, babysitting while doing homework) is the note or a
  topic of the active entry, not a second entry. The ring always adds up to the day.
- **Priority when entries overlap (`DayEntry.rank`):** what you said about that specific time (live, or told
  afterwards) > measured (Health) > scheduled (classes, calendar, things you said you'd do, all *assumed*) > a
  sensor's guess > a planned routine (sleep, dinner). Your one-off entries are kept non-overlapping when written
  (newer wins, the receipt says what was trimmed); everything else is settled when read, so rebuilding sensor
  entries never undoes a cut. Disagreements between a sensor and a schedule aren't settled silently: they become a
  question (Part N).
- **Assumed vs confirmed:** scheduled time, sensor time and planned routines are *assumed*: they count as what
  happened unless you say no. Confirming is a swipe (right = yes, left = no; one long swipe confirms a whole
  morning), on Home's timeline through the day and in the end-of-day review (B7, G.0b). No = skipped, then "what
  instead?". Planned task sessions are never assumed: they're tracked time only once you start them or say you did.
- **Gaps are honest:** time nothing explains is "Untracked" in the ring, not counted as free time. That's what the
  "what are you doing?" prompts (D4) shrink.
- **Derived, not duplicated:** the timetable, Health sleep and sensors produce entries through `EntryBuilder`
  (pure, in core), stored with their source so a rebuild is idempotent. Told and live entries are stored as they are.
- **Retroactive:** "between 3 and 5 I was studying chem at the library" → one told entry with place, category and
  topic. "Actually that was 3:30" edits it.
- **School timetable:** each period is its own repeating entry (School › Math, topic `math`; spares and lunch are
  `open`), assumed attended unless skipped, contradicted by location (Part N), or answered "no".

## Part C: Categories the AI files and keeps tidy

### C.0 Design

- `CategoryTree`: nodes `{id, name, parentId?, symbol, colour, group}`. Two or three levels. Seed (the AI grows it
  from there):
  - **Sleep**
  - **School** › each class from the timetable (Math, English …)
  - **Work** › Studying › subject (Math, Chem …); Extracurricular › Volleyball, Basketball …
  - **Essentials** › Shower, Meals, Getting Ready
  - **Travel**
  - **Free Time** › Making App, Friends, Gaming …
  - **Unproductive** › Scrolling (one category: which app doesn't matter)
  - **Untracked** (not a real category: time nothing explains)
- `group` (productive / essential / rest / unproductive) colours the inner ring and is what goals and rewards
  mostly use.
- **Topics cut across the tree.** "Math class" (School › Math) and "studying for the math test" (Work › Studying ›
  Math) both carry topic `math`, so "how much math?" adds them up, while "how much time in math class?" filters
  School › Math. The AI assigns topics, and the timetable and task subjects give them automatically.
- **Filing:** the box files each entry it creates, using an existing leaf if one fits and creating a new one if not
  (depth ≤3; the taxonomy pass tidies later). Entries that don't come through the box (timetable, sensors, buttons) are filed
  by `CategoryRules` in code: learned title → category rules from past filings and corrections, then fallbacks
  (period → School › class, any scrolling app → Unproductive › Scrolling, task → its task's category).
- **Corrections:** tap any slice or entry → move it to another category (a picker over the tree, or "New…"). That
  sets `filedByYou` and records a rule ("Shower" → Essentials › Shower). It's offered for all similar
  past entries ("Also move the 14 other 'Shower' entries?").
- **Background pass (`TaxonomyPass`):** one Sonnet call, at most once a day, when ≥10 entries were filed into new or
  thin leaves, or weekly. In: the tree with minutes and example titles per leaf, recent new leaves, your corrections.
  Out: merge, rename, move or create nodes, and remap entries. Applied through `ChangeLog` (Revert, Never Change
  This), ≤8 operations a run. It never changes entries or nodes you set or named, and never deletes a node that has
  time in it without remapping that time. Example: "Bball", "Basketball practice" and "Hoops" merge into
  Extracurricular › Basketball.

|---|---|---|
| C1 | `CategoryTree` (seed, add, rename, move, merge with entry remap, depth ≤3), `group`, symbols and colours from `CategoryPalette` (grayscale rules from v1.1 J still apply) | `CategoryTests` |

## Part D: Live tracking, start buttons, running notification, "what are you doing?"

### D.0 Design

- **Start and stop by text:** "starting shower" opens a live entry; "done", "done shower" or starting anything else
  closes it. "Started reading 10 minutes ago" backdates the start.
- **Start buttons (`QuickStart`):** the first time you start something by text that isn't a task, it gets a
  button. Buttons are ranked by how likely they are now (time of day, weekday, place, what you just finished) and
  show 4–6 at a time, with a "More" sheet. A button you don't use for 21 days drops out (still in More). Tapping
  one starts the entry with no AI call. Long press: rename, change category, remove.
- **Buttons and tasks are one idea.** Starting a task = starting an entry with its `taskId`. When there's a task
  for something today (a scheduled reading session, a recurring "Shower" the planner placed), its button is hidden
  and the task row's Start is the way in. Once something happens on most days at a similar time (Shower 21:30 on
  5 of 7 days), the planner offers it as a recurring task in the plan ("Shower ~21:30, 15 min"), so it gets a slot
  and doesn't collide with study. A plan where every button has become a task is the plan describing your whole day.
- **Running entry on screen:** Home shows the running entry at the top (title, category path, elapsed time, Stop,
  Switch). On the Lock Screen:
  - **First choice: a Live Activity** (ActivityKit, widget extension, **no App Group**: its data comes through
    ActivityKit). Elapsed time via `Text(timerInterval:)` counts by itself with no updates, plus title, category,
    place and goal progress ("Study 1h 10 of 2h"). Buttons Stop / Done via App Intents. DESIGN §14 lists this as
    possibly doable on a free account, so D5 is a spike first.
  - **Fallback if the extension can't be signed with Impactor / SideStore:** one passive notification (no sound),
    replaced on the same identifier. Pre-scheduled replacements every 10 minutes ("Shower · 20 min · Essentials ›
    Shower"), actions **Done** and **Switch** (text input → box). Cancelled on stop.
- **"What are you doing?" prompts** replace the "mystery gap" questions (`GapDetector`) and the "how did it go?"
  check-ins:
  - When nothing is running, no planned or timetable block covers now, and it's waking hours outside school:
    a notification "What are you up to?" with the 3 likeliest start buttons as actions plus **Reply** (text → box,
    through the outbox in A9, so a reply is never lost).
  - Timed by the **tracking goal: no untracked stretch longer than 30 minutes** (Part I). The first prompt comes at
    25 minutes untracked, so there's time to answer before the goal is missed; ask again every 30 minutes while
    nothing has started. Never during focus, wind-down or sleep. After 2 are ignored in a row, stop until the next
    day; a stretch that times out that way still counts against the goal, so the goal stays honest.
  - For time already past ("Nothing logged 3:10–4:30"), the same notification asks in the past tense; the answer
    becomes a told entry.
  - When a planned task session's start time passes and it isn't started, the "Starting now" nudge already covers it,
    so no second prompt.

## Part F: Ask anything about your time, get a chart

### F.0 Design

- The box gets a `query` output: a `TimeQuery` the app runs on the phone. The model never writes the numbers. Its reply
  uses placeholders (`{q1.total}`, `{q1.perDay}`) that code fills in, so the answer arrives in the same single call
  (no second round trip, and the conversation's `apply` continuity stays intact).

```json
{ "id": "q1",
  "range": { "last": "28d" },                       // or {from, to}, "this week", "school days in Oct"
  "filter": { "categories": ["school/math"],        // subtrees
              "topics": ["math"], "places": [], "tasks": [], "title": "",
              "sources": [], "weekdays": [], "hours": [] },
  "groupBy": "day|week|weekday|hour|category:1|category:2|category:3|topic|place|task|none",
  "measure": "minutes|share|count|avgPerDay|firstStart|lastEnd",
  "compare": "previous|none",
  "chart": "ring|bar|stackedBar|line|heatmap|number|table" }
```

- "How much time in math class over the past 28 days?" → `categories: [school/math]`. With the timetable known,
  that's periods × days attended, exact.
- "How much time doing math?" → `topics: ["math"]`, which adds math class, studying for math and math homework
  sessions, broken down by category in the chart.
- "When am I most unproductive?" → `groupBy: hour`, `group: unproductive`, heatmap. "Mood by time of day this month" →
  `measure: mood` over hours (G).
- `TimeQueryEngine` (core, pure): filter → clip to the range → group → measure; it reports coverage ("12 of 28 days
  have school periods; 3 were absences") so a thin answer says so. Validation: unknown categories, topics or places
  give a problem in the receipt, not an empty chart.
- `ChartCard` (App, Swift Charts): ring (`SectorMark`), bars, stacked bars, line, hour × weekday heatmap, big number,
  table. Charts are kept in the thread and can be **pinned** to Review ("Pin to Review").

## Part G: Home and Review

### G.0 Home, top to bottom

1. **Box** (unchanged).
2. **Running entry** (if any): title, category path, elapsed, Stop / Switch. Otherwise the **start buttons** row.
3. **Mood buttons:** five faces, always there. One tap logs it (and attaches it to the running entry), then the row
   shrinks to "🙂 at 3:10 · Change" for 2 hours and comes back. Replaces the 16:00 mood card.
4. **Today's ring** (the most important chart): inner ring = groups, outer ring = their level-2 categories, from wake
   until now, solid; the rest of the day **hatched** in the same ring, so it also shows what's left. The hatched part
   is a **forecast** (G.0a), not just the plan. In the centre: the selected slice's time, or awake time tracked. Tap a
   slice to see its leaves and entries (edit / recategorise from there). Goal ticks on the ring (Part I).

### G.0a The forecast you can beat

- `DayForecast` (core): how today would probably end, per category, if it goes like your recent similar days. It
  starts from your last 4 same-weekday days and the last 7 days of the same type (school day / free day), takes the
  median per category, and moves it toward today's plan for what's fixed (school periods, sleep from the planned
  bedtime). By hour, so at 4 pm it forecasts the rest of the day from what those days did after 4 pm.
- Before you've tracked anything it's the whole ring, hatched. As the day goes, real time replaces the forecast
  from wake onwards. Beside the ring, one line: **"On track to beat your usual: Study +25m, Scrolling −15m"**, or
  "Usual Tuesday: 2h 10 scrolling. You're at 1h 30 with 5 hours left." Only the 2 categories that differ most are
  named.
- **What counts as beating it:** more time in productive groups and in categories with an at-least goal; less in
  Unproductive, at-most goals and Untracked; sleep closer to your sleep need. Essentials and Free Time are neither
  (free time isn't something to cut).
- At bedtime: "You beat your usual Tuesday: 78 vs 64, +40m study, −30m scrolling", which is a reward moment (Part I). Missing it
  shows only the comparison, no red, no shame line.
- Needs ≥3 similar days. Before that the hatched part is the plan alone and the line is hidden.
- Why this should work: it's a comparison with your own past (kept in §12 F; comparisons with others are dropped),
  it's specific and close, so goal gradient applies, and it resets every day (fresh start). It's a prediction from
  the same week, so it isn't an impossible target.
5. **Up Next:** today's remaining tasks and sessions in order, each with Start, and swipe to Done / Clear / Delete.
   This is what "Rest of Today" becomes. Its one line on top is the only number there, and it means something:
   "Do these and you'll hit Study 2h and still have 1h 40 free before bed at 10:30." If that can't happen, it says
   so ("Not enough time for all of this: Gatsby moves to tomorrow").

Removed from Home: Rest of Today's three numbers (`DayNumbers`), `ScheduleCard`'s strip (the hatched ring covers it),
the `NowCard` / Start Focus (task Start does it), the check-in "Did you get to …?" card (D4), the place offer card
(E), the gap card (D4).

**Home is for during the day** (what's running, what's next, what to confirm); **Review is for the end of every day
and longer periods** (scores, insights, patterns, trends). You open Review every evening, so insights live there,
not on Home.

### G.0b End-of-day review (under a minute, the top of Review)

At wind-down, Review opens on today: the day's **untracked stretches** ("9:10–9:55: what was this?" with the
likeliest start buttons as answers), the **assumed stretches** not yet confirmed (swipe yes / no), then the **day
score** with its parts and the comparison with your usual day. Anything else goes to the box. It's the backstop
that gets every day to full coverage without prompts all day.

### G.0c The day score

`DayScore` (core, done): 0–100 from four parts, always shown with the parts so it's clear what moved it:

- **Tracked** (30): waking minutes accounted for (assumed time counts, Untracked doesn't).
- **Work** (40): task minutes done vs what the day's plan asked for, in any order.
- **Deadlines** (30): tasks due that day done by their due.
- **Scrolling** (negative): −1 per 6 minutes, at most −30. Scrolling is one category; which app doesn't matter.

Shown with the comparison (G.0a): "78 today, your usual Wednesday is 64: +40m work, −25m scrolling". The weights are
constants in `DayScore`, so they can be tuned once real days exist.

### G.1 Review, top to bottom

0. **Today** (G.0b, G.0c): untracked and assumed stretches to settle, the day score with its parts vs your usual,
   and today's patterns (Part P).
1. **Time ring** with Day / Week / Month, drill down (group → category → leaf → entries), and the previous period beside
   each slice ("Study 9h 10, ▲ 1h 20 vs last week"). Replaces As It Went.
2. **Goals** (Part I): this week's goals with progress.
3. **Life Metrics** (from v1.1 G), moved up and fed by entries. The four that matter most lead: **minutes tracked**,

## Part H: Tasks that learn from what you finish and drop

### H.0 Design

- **Track record:** every task ends as **done**, **dropped** (cleared or deleted unfinished, or past its due date and
  never started) or **partly** (some time logged, then dropped). Stored in `TaskRecord` (title, kind, category,
  topics, estimate vs actual minutes, how many times it was put off, created → due lead time, outcome). Kept even
  when the task itself is deleted.
- **Similar tasks:** `TaskHistory.similar(to:)` by category / topic + title words + kind, with a drop rate and the
  usual put-off count.
- **When you add a task** that's like ones you usually drop (≥3 similar, ≥50% dropped), the box reply says so in one
  line with the facts ("You've dropped 4 of the last 5 reading tasks; they're usually put off to after 9 pm"), and
  the task gets **attention**: an earlier, peak-hour first session, a 10-minute starter step (v1.1 Ovsiankina
  approach: name the next step, offer to resume), an optional alarm (asks, Part J), and it's mentioned first in Up Next.
  For a type you usually finish, nothing extra happens.
- The insight pass gets the track record (drop rate by category / kind / time of day) to tune the plan, within the
  existing `ChangeLog` limits.
- **Clear vs delete:** Clear = "not doing this" (counts as dropped; asks one optional "why?" chip: no time /
  not needed / didn't want to). Delete = "made by mistake" (not counted). Both are swipes.

### H.1 Reminders that motivate

The current ones are generic ("Starting now. Tap Start Focus, or snooze it 10 minutes.", "How did it go?"). New rules
for every task notification:

- Say **what exactly** to do first (the task's first step, written once by the box when it makes or splits the task,
  stored as `WorkItem.firstStep`), and **how long**: "Reading: just chapter 3's first 5 pages · 20 min".
- Say **what it leads to**: "then you're free until 9" or "that's Study 1h 40 of 2h today".
- Show progress made: "2 of 5 sessions done; the essay is half there".
- Fewer: one lead-up at most, no "hasn't started" follow-up if the student is in another running entry, no "How did
  it go?" (stopping the entry answers it).
- Actions: **Start**, **10 Min Later**, **Not Today** (sets `notBefore`, counts as put off).
- Tone rules from §12 stay: no shame, no streaks; a usually-dropped task gets the more concrete version, not a
  harsher one.

## Part I: Rewards that work

### I.0 Why the v1.1 rewards don't land

Celebrations fire on Done and on focus milestones, but there's nothing to aim at: no target, so no progress toward
it and nothing to close (goal gradient). Life Metrics sit below the Weekly Note and few people scroll there.

### I.1 Design

- **The first goal is built in: no untracked stretch longer than 30 minutes** in waking hours. It's what makes
  every other chart trustworthy. Shown as a count on the ring's centre ("Untracked: 0 gaps over 30 min today") and
  as a goal in Review ("no long gaps on 5 of 7 days"). School periods, sleep and anything inferred from a sensor
  count as tracked; Untracked is only time nothing explains. The D4 prompts are timed to it. A full day without a
  long gap is a reward moment (medium), and it's counted per month, never as a streak.
- **Beating the forecast** (G.0a) is the daily goal you don't have to set: it's there from the third similar day.
- **The day's plan, met in any order.** `Accountability.day`: what the plan asked of the day per task (snapshotted
  at the day's first plan, `AppState.dayPlans`) vs task time you tracked that day. A day is short only when the total
  is short; doing tomorrow's essay instead of today's reading isn't punished. A task finished early asked for no more
  than it took; a task you deliberately moved ("reading tomorrow") is deferred. A day can be *short* on work minutes;
  only a task can be *missed*, and only when its due time passes without it done (moving the due date isn't missing
  it). Missed deadlines feed the day score, Part H's drop learning and the Weekly Note.
- **Goals in time or count, per category, per day or week:** "Study 2h a day", "Extracurricular 6h a week", "Scrolling
  under 1h a day" (an upper limit), "Finish 3 tasks a day". The AI suggests starting goals from your first two weeks
  (just above what you already do: an achievable goal works better than a stretch one) and the box can set them ("my goal is 10
  hours of studying a week"). The insight pass may nudge a goal ±10% at most, only for goals it suggested, logged
  in Changes.
- **Shown where you look:** ticks on Today's ring, the line in Up Next, the Live Activity / running notification
  ("Study 1h 40 of 2h"), and Review → Goals.
- **Closing a goal is the reward moment** (feeds the existing `Rewards.respond`: closing a daily goal = medium; a
  weekly goal or a personal best = big; still ≤1 big a day). Under-limit goals (scrolling) celebrate at bedtime if
  kept.
- **Counts that can't break** (from v1.1 F): "hit Study goal on 18 days this month", never a streak.
- **Weekly Note leads with goals:** which ones closed, which moved, one suggestion.
- Life Metrics move up in Review (G1) and each goal gets a metric tile automatically.

## Part M: Everything a free Apple ID can reach, used for the time log

### M.0a Research on the three unconfirmed items (2026-11-19)

**Live Activities on a free Apple ID: likely yes.**
- SideInstaller v1.4.0's release notes: "Sideloading SideInstaller with a free Apple ID now uses 2 App IDs instead
  of 1, because the Live Activity needs its own." Its Live Activity shows on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic
  Island. That's a shipping app doing it on free accounts.
- Live Activities have no capability or entitlement row in Apple's table. In an Apple forum thread, a DTS engineer
  said a `com.apple.developer.live-activities` entitlement "isn't real"; the fix was to delete it. So all it needs
  is `NSSupportsLiveActivities` and a widget extension.
- **Cost:** one more App ID (the free limit is 10 per 7 days) and the 3-app limit is unchanged.
- **Watch out:** SideStore issue #1462 (closed 2026-09-03) said SideStore signed extensions without
  `CS_EXECSEG_MAIN_BINARY`, so they installed but never launched. Impactor and iloader signed them correctly. D5
  checks this after a SideStore refresh, since that's how the app is kept alive.

**App Groups between the app and an extension: don't rely on them.**
- Apple's table lists App Groups for free accounts, and SideStore itself uses one. But SideStore issue #1437:
  a widget couldn't read the app's App Group `UserDefaults` when sideloaded, though it worked from Xcode.
  Retested on SideStore 0.6.3, iOS 26.6.1, a free account: still broken. The issue is closed, but the last report
  says it isn't fixed. Issue #782: more than 3 App Groups breaks signing. Impactor's community docs contradict each
  other on whether it creates them.
- **Decision:** design every extension to work **without** shared storage. The Live Activity gets its content from
  ActivityKit. Its buttons and any Control Center / Lock Screen controls run `LiveActivityIntent`s, which execute
  in the app's process. So stateless controls ("Start Shower", "Stop") work. A widget that has to *read* app data
  (the ring) needs the App Group, so it's "later, if a test install proves it works".

**Focus filters (`SetFocusFilterIntent`): unreliable on their own.**
- No capability needed, and no report ties them to account type.
- But in Apple forum thread 761593, `perform()` runs when the app is in the foreground and **not when it's in the
  background**. A community reply cites WWDC22 "Meet Focus filters": background handling needs an App Intents
  extension (another App ID).
- There were regressions on iOS 18 beta (FB14715113) and macOS 26.5. One forum post says conforming the filter to
  `LiveActivityIntent` made it fire on iOS. Unconfirmed by Apple.
- **Decision:** the primary path is the **Shortcuts automation "When [Focus] turns on / off → Start / Stop
  Activity"**. v1.0 already relies on Shortcuts automations for scrolling, and they run without the app. The Focus
  filter is a bonus: it also lets each Focus carry "pause prompts". It's tried with the `LiveActivityIntent`
  conformance, and if it doesn't fire in the background it's dropped.

### M.0 What a free account can and can't use

Apple's "Supported capabilities (iOS)" table (developer.apple.com/help/account/reference/supported-capabilities-ios,
read 2026-11-19) lists these for a free **Apple Developer** account: **App Groups, Background Modes, Data Protection,
HealthKit, HomeKit, Inter-App Audio, Keychain Sharing, Maps** (MapKit, but no routing directions) and **Wireless
Accessory Configuration**. Everything else in the table is paid-only, including Push, iCloud, Siri (the SiriKit
entitlement), Communication Notifications (so no `INFocusStatusCenter`), Time Sensitive notifications, Family
Controls, Access WiFi Information (no SSID), NFC Tag Reading, WeatherKit, Journaling Suggestions, Game Center and
Sensitive Content Analysis.

It doesn't list the many permissions that need **no capability at all**, only a usage string and the user's yes:
Location (Always), Motion & Fitness, Calendars, Reminders, Photos, Camera, Microphone, Speech Recognition, Contacts,
Bluetooth, Local Network, Notifications (passive / active), AlarmKit, plus frameworks with no prompt: App Intents
(App Shortcuts, Focus filters, donations, Spotlight, controls), Vision (OCR), Foundation Models (Apple's on-device
model, iOS 26, Apple Intelligence phones), BackgroundTasks, Core Haptics, NWPathMonitor, UIDevice battery state.
Extensions (widget / Live Activity, notification content, share) need no capability but each costs an App ID
(10 per 7 days on a free account).

**Shortcuts personal automations are a free account's background events.** The Shortcuts app does the sensing (NFC,
Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, charger, sleep, alarms, app open / close, Focus, Action button, arrive / leave) and calls
Productium's App Intents. v1.0 already uses this for scrolling (`Friction`). v1.2 uses it for the time log.

## Part T: Telemetry and debugging, in every backup

The v1.1 backup could show *what* the AI did (call log, ops, problems) but not much of *why* the app did what it
did: why sleep was empty (Health permissions read as denied, but HealthKit can't report read access), why a plan
looked the way it did, which notifications fired and were answered. v1.2 keeps a bounded development log on the
phone, written into every backup:

- `AppState.telemetry` (`Telemetry`): events `{at, area, name, level, detail}`; ≤20,000 events, ≤60 days, values
  ≤4,000 characters. **Record anything that could ever be useful** (messages, ops as JSON, titles, coordinates,
  plan summaries): the app isn't released, and the log only leaves the phone in a backup you make. Areas: box, gap, planner, log, sensor, health, notification, alarm, intent, lifecycle, ai,
  storage, error.
- **Recorded at decisions and boundaries:** every box turn (ops, receipt lines, held ops, problems), every time the
  AI said it couldn't do something (`gap`: v1.3's backlog, replacing `gapNotes`), every replan (duration, warnings,
  shortfalls), every Health / location / motion read (count, error, last success), notifications scheduled /
  delivered / answered, alarm reconciliation diffs, intents and Shortcuts run, background launches, decode or save
  failures.
- `ExportBundle.diagnostics` (`Diagnostics.snapshot`): the first numbers to look at: entries by source, the running
  entry, coverage for the last 3 days (tracked / assumed / untracked), telemetry counts per area, the last error.
- A hidden Settings → Debug screen (long press the version): the last 200 events filtered by area, "Copy last box
  turn", and the diagnostics.
- The box schema gets an optional `gap` string: the model says what it couldn't do and why, instead of only telling
  you. It goes to telemetry, not to state.

## Decisions (made here, so sessions don't re-open them)

- **Swapped periods keep their own length** (2026-11-20): on even dates P2 Spare is 8:26–9:46, Math 9:46–10:58,
  English 10:58–12:11, Lunch 12:11–13:26. It's in the box prompt and the `timetable-odd-even` check.
- **One `Entry` type for all timed things** (Part 0). No new timed type is added in v1.2 or later; a new need is a
  field on `Entry`, a `Rule`, or a setting.
- **Time is the base.** Everything with a time is an `Entry`; tasks are deadlines that turn into time; sensors and
  you fill the past. A feature that can't be said as time, a task field, a Rule or a setting doesn't belong in v1.2.
- **Structure only where a weaker model needs it to be consistent:** refs, a handful of verbs, one way to edit an
  occurrence (skip, or detach into its own entry). Values stay open: free titles, category paths, any `DayRule`,
  notes. A field is added only when a real prompt needed it (`open`, `maxMinutesPerDay`, `notBefore`).
- **Trust the AI, ask the user.** No refusals; risky changes are held for Approve / Decline (0.3).
- **Assumed time counts.** It's accurate unless you say no; planned study is never assumed.
- **The day is judged by its total, not the order;** a task is missed only when its due passes undone.
- **Home is for during the day, Review for every evening and longer periods.** Insights and patterns go in Review.
- **Scrolling is one category.** Which app doesn't matter.
- **The day score** = tracked + work + deadlines − scrolling, always shown with its parts and next to your usual day.
- **Telemetry records anything useful** until release.
- **One active entry**; what's passive (music, babysitting) is a note or topic.
- **v1.2 is a clean break.** No migration from v1.1 state or backups; breaking changes to types, storage and the
  export format are fine. Don't add compatibility code.
- **The student's explicit words are trusted;** limits apply to what the AI does on its own (the insight pass).
- **Untracked is shown, not hidden in Free.** It's the motivation for the prompts and it keeps the data trustworthy.
- **Topics, not a second tree,** for cross-cutting subjects. One tree plus free topics covers "math class" vs "math".
- **Queries are run by code and the model only fills placeholders.** Same rule as v1.1: never trust model numbers.
- **Live Activity is a spike with a fallback**, not a dependency. The fallback notification is good enough to ship.
- **Recurring tasks from habits, not habit tracking.** No streaks or checklists. The planner just gives a habit
  its usual slot.
- **The AI asks before any new alarm** (J); quieter changes don't need asking.

## Risks and open questions

- **Prompt fatigue:** the 30-minute goal means up to ~2 prompts an hour when nothing is tracked. The back-off, the
  one-tap start buttons in the notification and sensor-inferred time counting as tracked are the safeguards. If
  it's still too much after a week, let location and Screen Time fill more gaps before asking. Don't loosen the
  goal without asking you first.
- **Forecast feeling like a judgement:** a bad day shown against "your usual" could discourage. Only the 2 biggest
  differences are named, missing shows no red, and free time is never something to "beat". If it still stings, show
  the line only when you're on track.
- **Battery from location:** visits + significant change are cheap; the background activity session during entries
  isn't. If E1's measured cost is too high, use the session only for entries without a known place.
- **Taxonomy churn:** a tree that reshapes itself every day is confusing. ≤1 run a day, ≤8 ops, your nodes pinned,
  Revert in Changes. If it still churns, make it weekly.
- **Free-account Live Activity:** the widget extension needs its own App ID (10 per 7 days). If installs start
  failing on the App ID limit, or a SideStore refresh leaves the extension unable to launch (#1462), drop to the
  fallback.
- **Part 0 is a big rewrite:** about 20 files touch `Commitment` and 16 touch `PlannedBlock`. It's a clean break
  (no data kept from v1.1), so the safety net is the rewritten tests on `Fx` and the everyday eval set at 100%
  after the schema change, not a comparison with v1.1.
- **Freedom in the exception lane** could produce a wrong but plausible change. The receipt shows the day-by-day
  effect, Undo is one tap, and the big ones wait for Approve. If one kind of exception goes wrong
  repeatedly in the evals, give it a worked example in the prompt, not a refusal.
- **Box schema growth:** categories, queries, goals and places add to the prompt, but Part 0 removes far more.
  Target: the box prompt at or under half of 12,010 characters (O6), measured in K5.
- **Timetable attendance:** assuming you attended every period overcounts on skipped classes. Location at school and
  day changes correct most of it; the query coverage line says "assumed from the timetable".
- **Too many permission prompts (M):** each source is asked only when it's first useful, with one line on what it
  gives. Nothing in Part M is required; the box alone still works.
- **Free-account App ID budget:** the app, the widget extension (D5 / M10) and any later extension each use one of
  the 10 App IDs per 7 days. Keep it to at most two targets in v1.2.
- **Privacy:** a full location and activity history is sensitive. It stays on the phone and in your backups only,
  nothing goes to the server except the compact context the box already sends (place names, not coordinates), and
  Settings → Privacy can delete it.
